# Content

A work answers its reader's question at that moment, and nothing else. Decide
the reader before the words.

## Decide, in order

1. **Who reads it:** a person, an agent, or both.
2. **Why:** the question they have right then. Each line answers one; a line
   that answers none is cut.
3. **When:** mid-task, deciding, or studying. This sets length and tone.
4. **Where:** the medium and what it limits. This sets form. The product's
   existing terms and the platform's conventions come first.
5. Apply the rules for every reader, then the matching branch.

## Every reader

- Claim only what is proved: no duration, outcome, or "nothing changed"
  without the evidence behind it.
- One term per thing, and the term the reader already knows.
- Too short is also a failure: a line whose subject or condition the reader
  must guess gets its words back.

## A person, mid-task (product text)

- A label or state name where one works; a sentence only where it does not.
- No mechanism and no reasons, unless the next action depends on them.
- Usual questions: what happened, did anything of mine change, what do I do.
- Tone scales with the stakes. When something of the reader's is at risk, the
  fact that settles it comes first. Feeling comes from specifics (the amount,
  the name), not from added warm words.
- Voice comes from the product's voice record (`onboard`'s brand item). With
  none, record it as missing and use the default: the product never speaks as
  a person (no "we", "I", "our"); try the wording without "you" first and
  keep it where dropping it loses whose thing it is or who must act.

## A person, deciding or studying (docs, change descriptions, announcements)

- The conclusion or the ask comes first.
- Reasons are the content here; length is allowed where the reader uses it.
- Narrative belongs in onboarding, help, and announcements, not in state text.

## An agent (briefs, prompts, handoffs, tool output)

- Completeness over brevity: exact identifiers, stated reasons, the boundary
  and the check for done. Tone and voice rules do not apply.
- `writing-skills` governs skill text, `handoff` handoffs, `tee-up` prompts.

## Both

The person's scan comes first, the agent's detail after it.

## Check

Shown only this work, its reader can say what it tells them and what to do
next; for product text, also whether anything of theirs changed. Authors,
reviewers, and guests run this on each changed state or section.

## Red flags

- Words written before the reader is named
- Product text: a second sentence explaining how the system works
- The product narrating its own effort or apologizing
- A time or outcome promised that the system does not bound
- A fragment whose subject the reader must guess
- New strings in a different voice from the ones beside them, unremarked
- Person rules applied to an agent's brief, or the reverse
